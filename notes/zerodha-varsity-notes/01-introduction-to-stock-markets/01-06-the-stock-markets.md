## How to Calculate Return

### Absolute Return

$$ \Big(\frac{\text{ending period value}}{\text{starting period value}} - 1\Big)  \times 100 $$

### Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)
$$ \Big(\frac{\text{ending value}}{\text{beginning value}}\Big)^
{\displaystyle\frac{1}{\text{no. of years}}}  - 1$$

### Example

Suppose you bought Insfosys share at 3030 and sold it in 3550 after two months.

Absolute return = $(3550/3030 -1) \times 100 = 17.16\% $

CAGR = $(3550/3030)^{(1/2)} - 1 = 0.0824 = 8.24\%$

If the same transaction was done in **6 months**: 

CAGR =  $(3550/3030)^{(1/(1/2))} - 1 = (3550/3030)^{2} - 1 = 0.3727 = 37.27\%$

Absolute return **stays the same**.

## Traders & Investors

**Trader** - spots an opportunity, initiates the trade and wants to exit the trade with a profit at the earliest opportunity. Usually short-term view of the markets.

- Day trader: Initiates and closes the position during the day
- Scalper: Type of day trader. Usually trades very large shares and holds the stock for less time to make a small but quick profit.
- Swing Trader: Holds on to the trade slightly longer - few days to weeks.

**Investor** - Buys a stock expecting significant appreciation in the stock. Willing to wait for the investment to evolve. The holding period runs into a few years.

- Growth Investors: Identifies companies expected to grow significantly.<br> Eg: Infosys, Hindustan Unilever, Gilette India stocks in 1990s.

- Value Investor: Identifies good companies irrespective of whether they are in growth or mature phase but beaten down significantly due to the short-term market sentiment hence maling a great value buy.<br>
Eg: COVID crash of March 2020 due to short-term negative sentiment, almost all good stocks were beaten down significantly around March/April and then did a V-shaped recovery in later months.
